PATON HALL

A delivery partner for work your mandate already names

A former mechanics garage in Hamilton, operating as an open industrial hub

The gap, in the City's own words

The Bayfront Industrial Area Strategy, approved by Council in September 2022, lists 47 actions. Action 42 is short-term:

"Form partnerships with local institutions to create space for start-up research and innovation opportunities working towards creation of a physical and digital campus. These partnerships will also work towards bridging education and employee training programs with businesses in the area."

The 2024 Action Plan Update (PED14117(e)) records where it stands: of 47 actions, 6 achieved, 13 initiated, 28 not started. Progress tracking was paused in early 2024. The theme "Creating an Advanced Manufacturing & Innovation Campus" sits at 0 achieved, 1 initiated, 5 not started, and the initiated item is pedestrian and cycling amenities.

To be precise about our claim: Paton Hall sits in Strathcona, outside the Bayfront study area, and has no standing in that strategy. We are pointing out that the City identified a function, called it short-term, and has not started it — and that we perform that function now, at no municipal cost.

What we are

One open room of roughly 2,000 sq ft in a garage that has been in mechanical use since about 1927. Concrete floor, garage height, a bay door large enough to drive a machine through, parking on site.

The people are the ones your intake misses: the millwright with an idea, the controls engineer between jobs, the founder with a working prototype and no company yet. They are building hardware, robotics, software and industrial products, and they are not enrolling or applying anywhere.

The model is membership-funded and volunteer-operated. Fifty-one members covers the entire operating cost. There is no funding gap to close before we can be useful to you.

What we can do that you cannot easily do
Reach people outside your intake They will not fill in a form. They will come by for a game of pool and stay for a build night.
Move at conversational speed A member proposes a seminar on Monday and it runs on Thursday. No course approval, no room booking committee.
Host physical work informally Build nights give people floor, power and hours for wrenching and robotics. Members bring their own tools. A garage handles that; a seminar room does not.
Report back honestly We keep a claims register with a status label on every claim, including the ones we have withdrawn. You will be able to see what worked.

What we want from you

Not money, in the first instance.

  • Recognition of training quality — a partner who can vouch for what is taught here, so seminars carry weight beyond our own say-so.
  • Referrals — the people who approach you and do not fit.
  • Speakers and instructors — your people, in a room full of ours.
  • A named contact who will take a call.

Certified training

We are in conversation with EPTAC about hosting certified IPC training in Hamilton — electronics inspection and standards to begin with, the credential the industry actually checks for. Nothing is signed and we will not describe it as more than that.

The context that matters to you: the Ontario Job Grant (relaunched May 2026, up to $10,000 per trainee) and Skills Development Fund Round 5 ($150M, opened June 2026) mean regional employers can often have this training funded. The demand exists and the money exists. The delivery point is what is missing.

The terms of engagement

The lease at our preferred site is one year, on a building that carries a redevelopment proposal — which is why the rent works. Everything we own comes apart and travels, and other Hamilton properties are being actively scouted.

We say that at the start rather than let you find it later. What we are asking you to back is a membership and a method, not an address.
